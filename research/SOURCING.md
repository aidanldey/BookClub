# Sourcing & Attribution Rules

These rules exist because the reader-voices section is the whole reason the fan
pages are worth building. One fabricated quote, discovered once, makes every
other quote on the site suspect.

## The hard rules

1. **Every reader quote is real and linkable.** If there's no working permalink,
   the quote doesn't ship. No composites, no "representative" comments, no
   lightly-reworded paraphrase presented as a quote.
2. **Quote accurately.** Trim with `…`, fix nothing else. Don't correct spelling,
   don't drop a qualifier that softens the take, don't stitch two sentences from
   different parts of a comment into one.
3. **Handles only.** Attribute to the public username. Never include a
   commenter's real name, location, workplace, or anything else identifying —
   even if their profile or comment reveals it. Don't cross-reference accounts.
4. **Skip anything vulnerable.** If a comment discloses someone's mental health
   crisis, abuse history, or similar, don't lift it onto a public fan page, no
   matter how moving it is. Grief and personal-loss comments are a judgment call:
   quote only when the comment is clearly written for a public audience.
5. **Honor takedowns instantly.** If a commenter asks to be removed, remove the
   quote — no verification, no negotiation. Keep the removal list in
   `data/quote-removals.md` so a re-research doesn't reinstate it.
6. **Use they/them** for commenters unless their comment makes their pronouns
   clear. A username tells you nothing about pronouns.

## Book quotes and copyright

Short excerpts for commentary and criticism are what these pages are for, but
"short" does the work:

| Status | Rule of thumb |
|---|---|
| Public domain (roughly pre-1930 US publication) | A paragraph is fine where it earns the space. Austen, Brontë, Melville, Shelley, Dumas, Hugo, Dostoevsky, Wilde. |
| In copyright | A sentence or two per quote. Never a full paragraph, never a poem or lyric in full, and cap the page at ~10 short quotes. |
| Translated works | Attribute the translator. A famous line is a *translator's* line — "Everything is permitted" reads differently in P&V than in Garnett. |

Always attribute: title, author, and (where relevant) translator and edition.

## What counts as a good source

**Strong:** high-engagement Reddit threads in book subs; Goodreads reviews with
substantial vote counts; long-running forum discussions; reader essays that
engage with the text.

**Weak:** publisher marketing copy; SEO listicles; AI-generated review-aggregator
sites; quote sites with no source citation (they're riddled with
misattributions — verify any quote you find there against the actual text).

**Never:** private communities, closed Discords, DMs, or anywhere a reasonable
person would expect their comment stays inside the room.

## Verifying a "famous" quote

Quote sites propagate errors. Before a line goes on a page, confirm it appears in
the actual text — search the book's full text, or find a reader citing chapter
and page. If a widely-loved line turns out to be misattributed or invented,
that's *more* interesting than the quote: put it in `commonly_misread`.

## Freshness

Re-run research on a page yearly, or whenever an adaptation lands — a film or
series shifts the conversation fast, and a page quoting only pre-adaptation
threads starts to feel stale. Keep `researched_at` current.
